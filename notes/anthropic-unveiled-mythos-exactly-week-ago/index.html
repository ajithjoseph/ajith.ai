Anthropic unveiled Mythos exactly a week ago on Tuesday, April 7, 2026.

It finds zero-day vulnerabilities in every major operating system.

It chains 4 bugs into a working browser exploit overnight.

It reverse engineers closed source binaries.

Engineers with no security background are waking up to finished exploits in their inbox.

If you work in observability, read that again.

For 24 years we have built stacks that assume the attacker moves at human speed.

Dashboards refresh every minute.

Alerts fire on thresholds tuned for last quarter's traffic.

Incident response runbooks assume a person is reading logs, forming a hypothesis, opening a ticket.

THAT ENTIRE MENTAL MODEL JUST GOT OBSOLETE.

When an AI can discover and weaponize a 27 year old bug in OpenBSD in a single run, your mean time to detect (MTTD) is no longer measured in minutes.

It is measured in how fast your telemetry pipeline can reason.

Logs, metrics and traces are not enough anymore.

You need SEMANTIC OBSERVABILITY.

Systems that understand what the data means, not just what it says.

A few things I am watching closely.

Synthetic monitoring is going to get reborn.

Not as scripted Selenium flows, but as adversarial agents probing your own surface the way Mythos would.

If you are not red teaming your production with AI, someone else is.

OpenTelemetry becomes the substrate.

The winners in this next wave are the platforms that can ingest everything and let a model reason across it in real time.

Dash0 is making that bet natively on OpenTelemetry.

Chronosphere is making it from the cloud native scale angle.

Both are reading the room correctly.

The legacy three pillar vendors are not.

The SOC and the SRE merge.

You cannot separate reliability from security when the same model can both find the bug and trigger the outage.

One pane of glass is no longer a vendor slogan.

It is survival.

I spent years building synthetic monitoring at PerfAgents and watching enterprise teams drown in signal without meaning.

Mythos does not create that problem.

It exposes how unprepared we already were.

The observability category is about to get rewritten.

The tools that survive will be the ones that think, not just the ones that store.

Who else is thinking about this? I want to compare notes.
