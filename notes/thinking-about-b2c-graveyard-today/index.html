I was thinking about the B2C graveyard today.

Most consumer apps don't die because they had a bad idea. They die because they lost the "vibe." And in 2026, the vibe is powered by AI agents.

We’ve officially entered the era of Intent-and-Generate.

The modern consumer doesn't want to browse your menu or click through your filters. They want to say, "Fix this for me," and have an agent do the heavy lifting.

But here’s the problem: Consumers are way less forgiving than Enterprise CEOs.

If a B2B tool glitches, you open a Jira ticket.

If a B2C agent glitches. You know what happens next? That customer is gone. Forever. Delete. 1-star review.

This is why B2C needs deep Observability even more than the Enterprise guys do.

In the B2C world, "uptime" isn't enough anymore. You need to see the "why."

Why did the agent give that specific response?

Where exactly did the reasoning chain break?

Was it a slow API, or did the model just lose the plot?

If you can't trace that interaction in real-time, you aren't running a business; you’re running a lottery.

I’ve realized during my morning runs that you can't improve what you can't measure.

You can't fix your form if you don't know where you're over-striding.

B2C is exactly the same. If you aren't using something like an OTel-native stack to watch your agents, you're just running blind and hoping you don't hit a wall.

The "Prime Sport" of the future isn't just about who builds the smartest agent. It’s about who has the best eyes on the ground.

So, for the B2C founders out there:

How are you actually watching your agents?

Are you still "praying" it works, or have you moved to a real Observability stack?

Drop your preferred setup in the comments.

I'm curious if anyone is actually making legacy logs work for this, or if we’ve all finally moved on.
